## When?

**When the round of calls is done** — or mid-round, if patterns are already screaming. Synthesis is where sessions become knowledge; skipping it means the loudest memory wins.

## Why?

**It's easier to find patterns when you can see everything.** Individual calls are anecdotes; synthesis makes them evidence. Done right, every finding carries its receipts — who said it, how often, how confident you are — which is what lets the team act on it without re-litigating.

## Step by step

### 1. Gather everything in one place

Once sessions are complete, bring all your notes into a spreadsheet, Miro board, or post-its. Everything, not the highlights — your memory of "the interesting bits" is already biased.

### 2. Group and compare

Cluster observations, compare across participants, find the themes and the differences. Keep the customer's words attached to every cluster.

### 3. Answer the research questions

Go back to the plan: do you have enough to answer each question? Mark what's answered, what's contradicted, and what's still open — the open ones seed the next round.

### 4. Label your confidence

For each finding, be explicit: how many participants, how strong the evidence, how much you're inferring. A finding heard once is a hypothesis; heard three times, a pattern.

### 5. Turn findings into bets

End with so-what: what should the team do, stop, or investigate because of this? Findings without implications get archived.
