## When?

**Anytime!** You can test your solutions at any stage. There are some differences between testing provocations and a fully functioning prototype, but the key steps and guidelines are the same.

## Why?

**To make sure we build the right thing, in the right way.** While we may be experts in our product, we are not our users. We don't know their wants and needs, and we can't predict how they'll respond to new ideas. We test with users to make sure we build things that are functional, understandable and usable.

## Step by step

### 1. Identify your research questions

Before you do anything: what are you trying to find out? Keep a record of all your research questions — broad or specific depending on your confidence level. They'll shape your discussion guide so you get the answers you need without wasting time.

### 2. Check your assumptions

Be aware of your assumptions and biases; we all have them. Do you favour one idea, or believe users already know how to do something? Write them all down so you can mitigate them in your research design.

### 3. Create a discussion guide

One of the most important steps to keep research fair, unbiased and consistent. Set the scene, write a script and tasks from your research questions, and think about how participants will navigate the prototype. Remind them we're not testing *them*, and have them speak their thoughts aloud as they complete the tasks.

### 4. In the session: watch what they do, not what they say

It's common for participants to say things that contradict their actions. Ignore general comments like "it feels intuitive" and look at what they're really doing. Ask for examples to clarify.

### 5. Don't fill the silence

Silence is awkward — people naturally fill it with words. Staying quiet (or just saying "mmhmm") gives users time to get past the obvious comments and give you richer insights.

### 6. Gather all your insights in one place

It's easier to find patterns when you can see everything. Gather your notes into a spreadsheet, Miro board or post-its. Group observations, compare them, find themes — and check back against your research questions.

### 7. Share your insights

Summarise everything in one shareable document: a research summary addressing your research questions, in-depth findings, and — most importantly — evidence in the form of customer quotes. Now tell your story.
