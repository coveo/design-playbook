## When?

**Anytime!** If the problem isn't clear, the team doesn't have shared knowledge, or there are too many "solutions".

## Why?

**Understanding the problem and collaborating on our best bet at solving it.** Design dashes (and sprints) are the fastest way to find out if a product is worth developing, if a feature is worth the effort, or if your value proposition is really valid. The outcome is a high-fidelity interactive prototype, tested by real users, with clear insights on where to go next — not a wireframe or paper prototype; it looks and feels like a real product.

**But… it's a sprint?** The original *Design Sprint* is a 4–5 day workshop. We adjusted it to fit our context:

| Design Sprint | Design Dash |
| --- | --- |
| Day 1 — interviews, problem definition, sketching | Session 1 — context & ideation |
| Day 2 — voting, storyboarding | Session 2 — voting & storyboarding |
| Day 3 & 4 — prototyping, research rounds | Done outside the dash, in the same order |

## Step by step

### Session 1 — Getting the context

Align on the problem: share existing client feedback, the output of a user story mapping session, technical debt, or demo how competitors approach similar issues. The key is a shared understanding before ideation. Defining the goal or sprint questions pre-meeting also works well.

### Session 1 — Ideation

Take notes, do crazy 8s, and ultimately create a **4-step sketch**. Rounds can be amended or repeated, as in <PlayRef slug="design-smash" />. The 4-step sketch should:

- ✅ have a cool codename
- ✅ show multiple pages/states
- ✅ carry as much detail and copy as possible
- ❌ include no explanation on the pages

### Session 2 — Voting

Upload the sketches to a Figma/Miro board, gallery-style. Everyone browses individually and votes on any small detail or concept they like. Then everyone votes on their favourite idea, while the master voter (usually the PM) casts two votes — together or split to combine ideas. It's not important whose idea "wins".

### Session 2 — Storyboarding

The winning idea moves to a separate space, and the process follows a <PlayRef slug="storyboarding" /> session: agree on the primary goal of the prototype and establish the steps around the sketch.

## What next?

**Prototyping.** Do you have agreement that what's going to be tested is our strongest bet? Turn the storyboard into an interactive prototype — <PlayRef slug="lofi-prototyping" /> if there's a lot to test or it's far from today's product, <PlayRef slug="hifi-prototyping" /> if you're confident it will resonate.
