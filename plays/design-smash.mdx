## When?

**After a Shaping Workshop… probably.** We need a precise and clear opportunity from a <PlayRef slug="shaping-workshop" />. We use a Design Smash to explore as many ideas as possible from as many people as possible — we need to get past the "obvious" ideas to find better ones. That means LOTS of ideas, including bad, silly and naive ones.

It works for the right *size* of opportunity — around 1–3 screens or 1–3 steps for the user. Too big and you'll end up with a broad, shallow set of ideas; too small and a Design Smash is overkill.

## Why?

**Write drunk; edit sober.**

- **We generate ideas separately and share ideas collaboratively.** Bad ideas are good — one bad idea can inspire someone else's brilliant idea. We're smashing ideas together, in a sort of imaginary CERN supercollider.
- **Quantity over quality.** It can feel uncomfortable to turn off the embarrassment filter, but it's a proven way to get to breakthrough ideas.
- **Diverse minds get diverse ideas.** Engineers, product managers, data scientists and designers all join in.
- **Making over talking.** We make concrete things rather than talk about abstract ideas, then choose the clearest and most promising to take forward — building shared understanding along the way.

## What do you need?

**The team needs:** a facilitator with a plan for the recipe you're going to use, and a Figma gallery ready to go with a "wall" and dots for voting later.

**Each participant needs:** a stack of blank paper, a chunky pen that photographs well, a camera to upload sketches, the Figma gallery open in their browser, and a readiness to scrape the barrel and share lots of ideas — the good, bad and ugly.

## Step by step

### 1. Overview of the problem (15 min)

Quick overview of the problem/opportunity plus the high-level ideas from the shaping workshop. Agree on a short summary and add it to the top of the Figma gallery. This is critical — we have to know the boundaries of the idea space we're exploring.

### 1b. Warm up (10 min)

A blank page is daunting. Break the ice with a fun warm-up: squiggle birds, 30 circles, telephone Pictionary, emoji self-portrait. It isn't part of the final outcome, so be as silly as you can.

### 2. Idea generation (remix these to suit)

- **Prime the pump** — gather inspiration from competitors and adjacent areas. What catches your eye? What's awful — could we do the opposite?
- **Crazy 8s** — fold a sheet into 8 sections, set a timer for 8 minutes, get 8 different ideas out. Scrappy is good; the interesting ideas are often at the bottom of the barrel.
- **Mini-gallery** — each person shares 3–4 ideas from their Crazy 8s (a broad range, not the "best"). Riff on each other's ideas, then do another round.
- **Take a break** — your subconscious needs time while your conscious brain is otherwise engaged. Walk away for 20 minutes; another wave of ideas will come.

### 3. Sketching (20–30 min)

Refer back to the context, then sketch your best idea in detail as a three-frame comic strip. Give it a catchy title. The sketch must be self-explanatory — no pitch allowed, but include as much copy as possible. Copy matters.

### 4. Coffee break and art gallery (10–20 min)

Stroll among the ideas. Dot the details and sketches you find interesting; post questions on post-its. It's not important whose idea "wins" — the idea that comes out is the team's idea.

### 5. Interpretation and feedback (~20 min)

The facilitator walks the gallery, describing what each sketch is trying to achieve — 60 seconds each, user hat on. No debate yet. Optionally the sketcher gets 15 seconds to clarify at the end.

### 6. Final vote

Read out the opportunity. Everyone places 3 dots on the sketches that best address it. The PM makes the final decision: either **3–4 provocations** (very different directions to explore) or **1 coherent solution** built from 1–3 ideas to storyboard and prototype.

## Common mistakes

1. **Not going deep, broad and bad** — one idea is a trap, two is a dilemma, three opens the door to four, five and six.
2. **Final sketch not self-explanatory** — DO NOT PITCH IT. Seriously. UI is made of words, boxes and arrows; we need to see UI in the gallery.
3. **Goal and steps not clear** — the facilitator needs to be clear about where we're going, why, and how many rounds.
4. **Not finishing / rushing** — without a satisfying dot-vote decision at the end, everyone leaves feeling a bit… meh.

## What next?

**Divergent ideas, no clear winner?** You have unresolved questions or competing assumptions — a designer turns the biggest ones into <PlayRef slug="provocations" />.

**Similar ideas from everyone?** The winning sketches become the seed for a <PlayRef slug="storyboarding" /> session, which becomes a low-fidelity prototype.
