## When?

**It kicks off every larger feature or problem.** Most roadmap initiatives that involve design begin with a shaping workshop. It helps us all understand the problem space, define the constraints, and begin to explore how we might solve it. One of the key outcomes is a path forward for research, and explicit constraints of what's in scope and out of scope.

## Why?

**Shaping the problem and defining a path forward.** It gives the whole team time to sit down and explore the problem and why it is a problem, discuss technical and design solutions, and define a clear path forward for kicking off the project. Smaller BAU design changes don't need one.

Shaping is different from normal specifications: we don't start with all the stuff we want to do and then estimate how long it will take. We start by setting how much time we want to budget, then reframe the problem, and mainly decide what we're *not* going to do.

**Involve engineers early.** Engineering having a seat at the table helps us stage the approach to building a new capability, and surfaces technical approaches that enable possibilities — or introduce constraints — we may not otherwise have thought of.

## Step by step

### 1. Introduce the opportunity

The product manager runs the team through what we know so far. Why this? Why now? They share supporting evidence — current customer behaviours, pain points, requests — the desired outcome, how big a bet we want to make, and what's in and out of scope.

### 2. Break out into groups

Ideally two or more groups, each made up of engineers plus a designer or product manager for customer context. Separate groups give us different ideas and perspectives on how to approach the problem.

### 3. Write a pitch

Discuss what we think the problem we're *really* solving is, then explore concepts for how to address it. Capture questions and assumptions along the way (and what problems we're not solving). Explore the technical implications, how we might build it, how long it might take, and how we'll detect the outcome we're looking for — what behaviours and metrics will tell us it works. Add *pivot switches*: signals that indicate we're on the wrong path. This is also a good moment to sequence the work into smaller releases.

### 4. Share with the group

Each team takes it in turns to share their pitch, and we all ask questions and pull the ideas apart. Sometimes all the ideas overlap a lot; sometimes everyone's gone wild.

## What next?

**Discuss and align.** After all pitches are presented, discuss which approach feels best to move forward with. Then take the precise, clear opportunity into a [Design Smash](/#/plays/design-smash) to ideate on our best bets to tackle it.
