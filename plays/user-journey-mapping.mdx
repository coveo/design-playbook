## When?

**Early, when needed.** This session gives the team shared knowledge around an existing user journey, so it applies to many situations: when a team is newly formed, when the team is exploring a new space, or when there are lots of UX unknowns.

## Step by step

### 1. Choose a journey

The journey can vary from high-level to a particular one (e.g. *creating a campaign* vs *user views a health issue*).

### 2. Define the users involved

Start with the users involved in the process. If there are multiple, understand what overlaps and what doesn't to decide between separate journeys or a unified one — e.g. merchandiser, client developer, customer support.

### 3. Journey phases

Every journey has phases, whether high-level or specific — *Notification → Investigation → Resolution*. It's up to the facilitator and team to find the granularity that works.

### 4. Start sketching

Write down the steps, starting with the first phase. Make things granular enough to establish a story of what actions are happening.

### 5. Add emotions!

Highlight the ups and downs of the journey. It helps everyone understand at what stage the user is delighted or frustrated.

### 6. Opportunities

Think of these as questions we need to ask ourselves based on the evidence so far: "How can we build trust when everything's running smoothly?" or "What level of detail is *enough* for key stakeholders?"

## What next?

**Explore the opportunities.** Evaluating your opportunities helps you understand what could be prioritised next. Once you're confident about the best problem to tackle, do more of the understanding-the-problem plays.
