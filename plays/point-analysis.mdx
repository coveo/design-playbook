## When?

**1st step of a project.** When you're starting a project, it's always worth taking the time to gather all the internal information and documentation related to the problem you're designing for. The last thing you want is to repeat efforts.

*Start at: Coveo@Coveo, PowerPoints, Confluence pages, RCA reports, Tableau dashboards, Salesforce, Gainsight, SupportLogic, Slack threads…*

## Why?

**State what is known and identify the gaps.** Every department generates knowledge in its day-to-day work, but rarely does anyone gather cross-department knowledge to solve common problems with a holistic view. The outcome of a P.O.I.N.T. analysis is to (1) state what is known and work from that, and (2) identify the knowledge gaps and generate working hypotheses.

## Step by step

### 1. Gather data

Put on your investigator hat and gather all the documentation you can find about the problem. Don't forget to look outside your department — you might be surprised by what you find (service stats, win/loss workbooks, support RCAs, UX research, voice-of-customer feedback, competitive research…).

### 2. Unpack data

Go through the documentation one piece at a time and unpack what are:

- **P** — Problems (business, user, product, service)
- **O** — Opportunities for improvement
- **I** — Insights
- **N** — Needs (user)
- **T** — Trends (competitive, statistics, etc.)

Print everything and run a collaborative cut–glue–post-it workshop, or analyse digitally and paste into a spreadsheet. Laid out like this, the data is easier to digest.

### 3. Data overview

Synthesise your data and identify the main problems, opportunities, insights and needs that are well documented and known. When complete, the gaps will come to you easily — and you'll know where to go next.

## What next?

**Explore the opportunities.** Evaluating your opportunities helps you understand what could be prioritised next.
