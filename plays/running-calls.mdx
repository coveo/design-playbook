> **Playbook vs Confluence** — this play covers the *tactics* of facilitating a call. The operational side (scheduling, co-hosts, recording tooling, note-taker logistics) lives in the Design Confluence.

## When?

**Every customer call** — discovery conversations, prototype tests, provocation reactions. The goal changes; the facilitation craft doesn't.

## Why?

**The feedback is only as honest as the session lets it be.** People are polite, suggestible, and eager to be helpful — every facilitation habit here exists to defeat one of those instincts. Treat it like any other relationship: be respectful of their time, and always listen first.

## Step by step

### 1. Set expectations

Open with the framing from your discussion guide: you're testing the idea, not them; honest reactions help most; their feedback genuinely shapes what gets built — say so. Ask consent before recording, the first time, every time, and explain why.

### 2. Watch what they do, not what they say

It's common for participants to say things that contradict their actions. Ignore general comments like "it feels intuitive" and look at what they're really doing. Ask for examples to clarify.

### 3. Don't fill the silence

Silence is awkward; people naturally fill it with words. Staying quiet — or just saying "mmhmm" — gives them time to get past the obvious comments and give you richer insights.

### 4. Never rescue, never pitch

Don't explain what you'd build, and don't save them from a struggle with the product — the struggle is the data. The moment you pitch, you're collecting compliments.

### 5. Keep questions open and behavioural

No leading questions (anything inviting a yes/no), no hypotheticals, no subjective comparisons — "is this easier?" gets answers people can't explain. If they volunteer "easier", ask why.

### 6. Capture verbatim

Take notes as close to a transcript as you can; don't interpret or summarize while capturing. The customer's own words are the evidence everything downstream runs on.

## What next?

Don't wait for the round to finish — if a call surfaces something glaringly obvious, fix it and probe it in the next session. When the round is done, take your notes into <PlayRef slug="synthesising-research" />.
