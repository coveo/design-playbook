## When?

**When you need breadth, not speed.** A cross-team project where one or two loud voices shouldn't steer the outcome, and you want several people saying the same thing before you change direction. It shines across time zones: when London has lunch as Montreal wakes up, no slot works for everyone.

If you need a decision this afternoon, or only two opinions, book the room.

## Why?

**Nobody has to find a slot.** People review in their focus time, look properly before they speak, and don't end up agreeing with whoever spoke first. Introverts and non-designers get room to think, with no jargon thrown at them. And anyone in the channel can follow along: a 5-minute video is short enough that even leadership might watch it.

## What do you need?

**6–8 reviewers, cross-functional on purpose**, to keep you out of the designer echo chamber: a fellow designer, a design system designer, a PM from another team, an engineer, someone customer-facing, and leadership where it matters. More will join unasked, so make everything readable by anyone in the channel.

**A board** in Miro or Figma, **a short video walkthrough**, a shared channel and **5 working days**.

## Step by step

### 1. Build the board (before day 1)

Walkthrough video first, then the context: the problem (an example, a link to the initiative), what research told you, and the one question this crit must answer. Then one area per topic, laid out as a flow of screens and A/B/C variants, not a prototype: people can't comment on a prototype, or on each other's comments.

![Example async crit board on campaign scheduling: a walkthrough video, then context (the problem, research, the goal of the critique), then three topics with flows and A/B/C options](covers/async-crit-board.webp)

### 2. Set the rules (before day 1)

Ask for comments, not sticky notes. Threads keep the conversation going, and people get notified when a colleague disagrees.

### 3. Record a walkthrough (before day 1)

5–10 minutes, made as if the video were the only thing people will see: problem, research, solution. Any screen recorder works; pin it to the board.

### 4. Check access, then post (day 1)

> **Before you post** — check that every reviewer can comment on the board and watch the video. Finding out on day three is the most common way these crits stall.

In Miro, open **Share** and set the role for anyone in the company to **Commenter**:

![Miro's Share dialog with the role for anyone in the company set to Commenter](covers/async-crit-board-access.png)

Then post in the channel: tag your reviewers, give the problem and the solution in a sentence each, say exactly what to do, and end with the video and the deadline.

![Example kick-off message in Slack: an async crit on campaign scheduling that tags the reviewers, states the problem and solution, lists three things to do, gives the deadline and attaches the walkthrough video](covers/async-crit-shareout.png)

### 5. Nudge half-way (day 3)

Nothing much happens in the first two days; people have lives. Reply in the thread, tag whoever hasn't taken part, and ask them to say if they can't. Expect someone to ask for an extension.

![Example half-way nudge in the Slack thread: replies from people who are done, then a reminder that tags the three who haven't taken part yet, restates the deadline and asks them to say if they can't](covers/async-crit-followup.png)

### 6. Review with your cross-functional partners (day 5)

Go through the comments together: what people liked, what they hated, which variant won, and what needs more exploration.

### 7. Close the loop (day 5)

Thank everyone in the thread and share what you're keeping, revisiting and following up on. People who see their comments land say yes to your next crit.

![Example closing message in the Slack thread: a thank-you with a summary of what's being kept, what's being revisited, what needs follow-up, and the next steps](covers/async-crit-close.png)

## Common mistakes

1. **A two-day deadline** — people need time to actually do this.
2. **Finding access problems on day three** — check before you post.
3. **A 20-minute video** — nobody is watching that at any speed.
4. **No clear question** — the feedback scatters across everything.
5. **Skipping the nudge** — silence isn't consent; it's usually a full inbox.
6. **Going quiet at the end** — no summary, no second crit.
