## When?

**At direction moments.** A new problem space, a strategy reset, or an opportunity too big for one roadmap line. The tell: the team ships increment after increment, but nobody can say what it adds up to.

## Why?

**A vision deck gets nods; a visiontype gets believers.** A visiontype is a prototype of the product vision — [SVPG's term](https://www.svpg.com/the-power-of-visiontypes/) — and it works because people align around a future they can *see*. The PARC demo didn't hand Apple a product; it made a future imaginable. Alignment first, innovation after.

**The vision is where discovery starts, not ends.** Most of the work — and nearly all the innovation — comes after it. The visiontype's only job is to make everyone want to do that work in the same direction.

## What do you need?

A PM + designer pair with a real opinion about the future, the evidence from discovery, one to two weeks, and <PlayRef slug="prototyping-with-agents" /> to build it fast.

## Step by step

### 1. Anchor on a future moment, not a feature list

Pick the two or three moments in a future user's day that would prove the vision. Write them as a story first — if the prose doesn't hold, polish won't save it.

### 2. Choose the flag distance

Far enough that today's constraints don't apply, near enough to stay believable. Three years of ambition beats ten years of science fiction.

### 3. Build it like it's real

Real components, real-feeling data. It's theatre: polish what the story walks through, fake everything else without apology.

### 4. Wrap it in the narrative

The demo *is* a story: today's pain, then the future moment. Rehearse it — a visiontype presented badly is just a weird unshippable prototype.

### 5. Show it to recruit, not to test

Playbacks with leadership, teams, candidates. Watch for the alignment signal — "when can we have this?" — and capture what it provokes.

### 6. Mine it for the roadmap

Decompose the vision into bets. It earns its keep when a <PlayRef slug="shaping-workshop" /> opens with "this is the first slice."

## Common mistakes

1. **Treating it as a commitment** — it's a compass, not a contract. Date-stamping the flag schedules disappointment.
2. **Falling in love with the enabling tech** — love the problem; discover whether the tech can carry it.
3. **Confusing it with a first release** — nobody builds the visiontype. You discover your way toward it.
4. **Vision without delivery** — Xerox made the visiontype; Apple shipped the vision. Sharing recruits believers *and* briefs competitors.
5. **Playing it safe** — if it looks like next quarter's release, it's a roadmap slide in a costume.

## What next?

The riskiest beliefs inside the vision become <PlayRef slug="provocations" /> to test; the first slice becomes a <PlayRef slug="shaping-workshop" />. Revisit it on a cadence — a flag nobody looks at is just a stick in the ground.
